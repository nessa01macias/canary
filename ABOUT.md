# What is Canary?

**Canary is a jurisdiction-research API for AEC pursuit teams.**

Before an engineering or architecture firm can respond to a public infrastructure
RFP, someone has to research the site and the jurisdiction: the planning act,
the zoning by-law, what's already been approved nearby, the submittal
requirements for that specific city. At a firm like AECOM that research runs
2–3 months and ties up three or more senior staff — on a $1B+ pursuit, 6 to 12
months. The information is entirely public. It is just fragmented across
municipal websites, PDFs, zoning by-laws, submittal registers, and phone calls
to city staff, differently in every jurisdiction.

## How Canary answers it

We read the public record so a pursuit team doesn't have to. Planning acts,
zoning by-laws, permits, nearby approvals, and submittal requirements become
one structured, cited answer, delivered as an API and an MCP server that
plugs directly into the tools a pursuit team already runs — Claude, Copilot,
ArcGIS. No new interface to learn: the model on the other end drafts the
proposal natively, we're the thing it calls to get the jurisdiction facts
right.

We compress the **gathering** of the public record from weeks to days. We do
not claim to replace the genuinely private tier of pursuit knowledge —
evaluator preferences, incumbent relationships, the pre-RFP whisper network,
confidential clauses. Being honest about that boundary is part of how we earn
trust with the people who've done this research by hand for years.

## Why you can trust it

Every answer traces to the exact code section, version date, and source URL
behind it. That is the entire pitch against "just ask ChatGPT": teams already
do that, report roughly 85% accuracy from prompting general AI themselves, and
have no way to check any individual answer against the record. We show our
work on every field.

## Who this is for

**Large AEC consultancies and consortium delivery partners** responding to
public infrastructure, transit, and city-development RFPs — firms like AECOM,
Jacobs, WSP, Stantec, Arup, Bechtel, Turner & Townsend, Mott MacDonald.

**Mid-size regional engineering and environmental consultancies** in parallel
— they bid constantly, feel the research burden directly, and can say yes to a
pilot without a six-month procurement cycle. Environmental and regulatory
consultancies are a particularly strong fit: their deliverable literally *is*
cited jurisdiction research.
